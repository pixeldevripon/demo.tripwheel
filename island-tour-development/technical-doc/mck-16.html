Klein Curaçao hub, section by section

The hero of the Klein Curaçao hub. This is the page paid traffic lands on, so it is the first thing a visitor sees of Island Tours, and the only screen that has to carry the promise and the date field at the same time. Nothing here is a new component: every style is lifted from mck-06 v2. Eight things change. The four facts sit two per row on mobile so the date field and the button reach the first screen, the spacing around them is regrouped so the button stands apart from the text above it, the duration is corrected, and the price is rounded with a currency icon that follows the currency, and the intro under the hero gets its three levels back instead of one bold slab. Two sections further down the page are here too: the comparison table, which mck-06 v2 never drew for a phone, and the button that closes the Discover block. What has to change is listed at the bottom of this file.

1 · Mobile

The hero has one job on a phone: get the date field and the button into the first screen. Four facts stacked in a column push them down.

Four facts, four rows. The only two things a visitor can act on, the date field and the button, sit below all four of them. The duration also folds the 48-hour overnight charter into a day trip, the euro price is a raw conversion, and its icon is a dollar sign.

3 · The intro under the hero

Same words on both sides, so the only thing that differs is the weight. mck-06 v2 sets the opening sentence as its own paragraph in the display face, one step larger and bold, and everything after it at normal weight. Measured: the intro is 95 words. In the 720px desktop measure that is 214px, about eight lines. In a 339px phone measure the same words cost 431px, twice as much and more than half a screen once the nav and the hero are counted. That is why desktop shows all of it and only mobile clamps: one rule, keep the intro proportionate to the screen, two different results because line count scales with measure.

As built

The opening sentence is merged into the paragraph behind it and the whole thing is set bold, so about sixty words of body copy arrive as one heavy slab. Emphasis is relative: when all of it is bold, none of it is.

Why Klein Curaçao

The best beach in Curaçao isn’t on Curaçao. Klein Curaçao lies 10 km offshore, a flat, uninhabited island where nothing stays except a lighthouse and the sea turtles that return here to nest every year. No shops. No signal. Just one of the longest stretches of white sand in the Caribbean. A full day to disappear. We’ve been on every boat that makes the trip. We’ve never met anyone who regretted going.

The crossing sails against the trade winds. Some mornings it’s glass-smooth. Others, the catamaran earns its way there. Worth it. All of it.

On mobile the clamp cuts this block mid word above a Learn more link, so the hook sentence ends up inside the truncated text.

Proposed

Three paragraphs and three levels: the heading, then the one sentence lead-in in the display face, then normal body copy. The lead-in is a different register, not just a heavier one, which is why it takes the display face and not body text at bold. No clamp here: at 214px a Learn more would buy back a fifth of a screen in exchange for a click, and it would hide the shortest, hardest working sentences in the block.

This section fills that gap. The frames below are the specification; the numbered list under them says what each part has to do.

Two swipe positions of the same table. Two things stay behind on the first trip and never follow the swipe: the blue block behind the first tour name, and the price with the Book button. From the second trip onward the visitor reads one boat, is quoted another, and the button books the boat that is no longer on screen. How the mobile comparison has to behave

Eight points. The first six are numbered onto the proposed frame above; the last two are behaviour a still drawing cannot show. All of it applies to the Comfort table and the Adventure table in the same way.
  1. 1Two tables, never merged. Comfort trips and Adventure trips each keep their own table and their own group band, as on desktop. The band spans the table and scrolls with it.
  2. 2The row labels stay put. What stands out, Boat & group, Crossing, Breakfast, Open bar and Free cancel hold their place while the trips move past them, so the visitor always knows which line they are reading. They take less width than they do today, which is what makes room for a second trip.
  3. 3No trip is marked as chosen. Every tour name is set the same way. The build puts a blue block behind the first tour name in both tables and never moves it, which reads as a recommendation nobody made, on a page that says in the block above it that its picks are not paid placements. Remove it. A highlight here would have to be earned and labelled.
  4. 4Two trips on screen at once. A comparison needs a second thing to compare against; one trip at a time is a browse. A swipe moves by one whole trip and comes to rest on whole trips, never halfway between two.
  5. 5Price and Book live in the trip’s own column. They sit at the foot of that column and travel with it, so the amount and the button always belong to the boat directly above them. Not a bar under the table, not a bar that follows the swipe: a bar that can fall out of step is the defect being fixed here.
  6. 6The visitor can see there is more, without doing anything. A slice of the next trip stays visible at the edge, and a position indicator under the table shows where they are and how many trips there are. Both tables carry both. Today the Adventure table shows a scrollbar and the Comfort table shows nothing at all, and on a phone a scrollbar is not an affordance: it only appears once you are already scrolling, which is after the moment it was needed.
  7. 7Both tables behave identically. Whatever is chosen for width, swipe and indicator applies to Comfort and Adventure in the same way, including which trip is on screen first.
  8. 8The table shows once that it moves. When a table first scrolls into view it nudges a short distance sideways and settles back, so the trips are seen moving before the visitor decides there is nothing more to look at. Three limits on it. Once per table per visit, not every time it passes the screen. Never while a finger is on the table, and never a movement tied to how far down the page you have scrolled: one hint, then it is still. And skipped entirely for anyone whose device asks for reduced motion. If this is adopted it is how every horizontal scroller on the platform announces itself, not a one-off here.
Unchanged on mobile. The rows keep their desktop order and their desktop wording. Included and not included still read as they do on desktop, a check for included and the words Not included where it is not, never a blank cell. Columns are named by tour title, never by operator name. The section keeps its heading and both sub lines.

The words, and why they changed

HeadingYou can only get there by boat.Ready to go? A question the visitor can answer no to, and it says nothing. This says the one thing that is true of this island and of nowhere else on the platform: it is uninhabited and 10 km offshore, so the boat is not a preference, it is the only access. That is what turns reading about the place into picking a boat.
Line under itNine of them leave from Curaçao, most days.Answers the two doubts that follow straight away, is there choice and can I go soon, with facts rather than pressure. “Most days” is accurate and stays: not every boat runs daily, one is weekends only, and the register has to be able to say so.
ButtonPick your boatBook your trip books nothing, it scrolls to the trips grid. This says what actually happens next. It is also the third control on this page pointing at the same anchor, after the sticky nav and the link under the picks block, which is worth settling before this ships.
The numberbound to the registerNine is what the trips grid renders today. It has to come from the same count, or the line drops the number. A hardcoded nine goes stale the first time a boat is added, on the page paid traffic lands on.
Keep it to thisone line, one fact, one buttonNo icon, no third line, no second button. The page is already long and this block earns its place by ending the section, not by adding to it. Use one of the two tints that already exist; the comparison tables already carry a coloured band.
What changes in the hero, and what does not
  • Four facts go two per row on mobile, wrapping back to a column when a translation is too long for half a screen. The date field and the button move up by roughly the height of two lines, which is what decides whether they are in the first screen on a small phone.
  • Duration reads "Full day (6-9h)". The 48 in the build comes from the overnight charter, which is not a day trip and should not set the duration in the hero of the page it does not belong to.
  • The price rounds up: "From €121", never "From €120.50". Same rule everywhere a converted price appears.
  • The currency icon follows the currency, a euro glyph on euro amounts and the existing dollar glyph on dollar amounts. Today a euro amount carries a dollar sign.
  • Restored from the hub mockup: "BBQ lunch included" and "Daily departures", where the build shortens both.
  • The shape of the date field and the button stays exactly as it is. Do not unify it with the round search pill from the home and destination heroes: that pill is a site search that docks under the nav on scroll, this is a date filter for the page you are already on. They are the same family, not the same control.
  • The hero is missing its Share button. mck-06 v2 carries one: a translucent white pill with the share icon and the word Share, absolutely positioned in the top right corner of the hero image, the same control the collection page puts in the same corner. The build dropped it. It is the only way to send this page to the person you are planning the trip with, on the page most likely to be planned with somebody else. Shown in the proposed heroes above.
  • The intro under the hero is three paragraphs, not one bold block. The opening sentence, "The best beach in Curaçao isn’t on Curaçao.", is its own paragraph in the display face, one step larger and bold; everything after it is normal weight body text. The build merged that sentence into the paragraph behind it and set the lot bold. On mobile the clamp stays, the intro has to be short there: the lead-in renders in full, then about two lines, and Learn more runs on inline directly after the ellipsis rather than sitting on its own line, so the hook is never inside the cut and the link never costs an extra row. If those two words stay, the link has to go somewhere, to the Discover section for instance, because that is what they mean everywhere else on this platform. If it expands in place instead, it needs a different label and a way to close again. Desktop carries the intro in full and never clamps: the same 95 words are 214px there against 431px on a phone, so a Learn more on desktop would trade a click for a fifth of a screen. Section 3.
  • The comparison table on mobile is specified in full in section 4, drawing and numbered list. Two things there are defects, not preferences. The price and the Book button belong to the trip they sit under: today one bar sits under all three and does not move when the table is swiped, so the visitor reads one boat and is quoted another, and the button books the boat that is no longer on screen. It is checkable on the live page, the same euro amount appears under two boats whose dollar prices differ. And the blue block behind the first tour name in both tables never moves either, so one trip is permanently marked as chosen on a page that says one block higher that its picks are not paid placements. The rest of section 4 says how the mobile version has to work: two trips on screen at once, row labels that stay put, a swipe that lands on whole trips, price and button inside each trip’s own column, a position indicator under both tables, and both tables behaving the same. A pinned bar that tries to follow the swipe is the same defect with more moving parts; a cell that lives in the column cannot get out of sync.
  • The button that closes the Discover block gets a band instead of more colour. Today it is an outline centred in white with nothing above it and nothing holding it, and it is the only outlined control on a page where every other orange control is filled, so it reads as less than a button. Filling it is the wrong fix: it would outrank the six real Book buttons in the comparison above while doing less than they do. Give the section an ending instead, one tinted band carrying one line, one fact and the button. The words change too: “Book your trip” books nothing, it jumps to the trips grid, and that grid is already the first item in the sticky bar, so the label has to say what it does and the destination is worth settling. The fill is `cta-deep`, not `cta`, for the contrast reason that applies to every primary button on the platform. Section 5.
  • The Select date controls in the hero, above Trips and above Private charters are one page wide filter, not three competing ones: set the date anywhere and it is set everywhere, because the page is long and availability is thin. Nothing to change in the design. Worth verifying in the build that they really do share one state, and that once a date is chosen every instance shows it instead of still reading Select date.
