MCK-20 · Booking flow decision

Operator conditions at booking

Your call of August 13: a tour whose operator attaches its own conditions gets a required checkbox before the booking can be committed. Per tour, never platform wide. This file answers the three open questions: are the two cases really the same kind of thing (section 1), where does the checkbox land (section 2), and modal or its own page for the readable document (section 3). Every legal sentence in here is placeholder: the legal workstream D1/D2 owns the words, this file owns the pattern. One live demo runs in section 2 and section 3.

1 · Two flavors, and they are not the same thing

Powerboat Caribbean has a real conditions document, the old site even gave it its own page. The dolphin tours have a checklist the live build happens to label Terms & Conditions. Put side by side, they are different legal objects and earn different patterns. Both checkboxes below are tappable.

Flavor 1 · Operator terms, with a document

Powerboat Caribbean, the operator behind the Klein Curaçao Speedboat Day Trip and the West Coast Speedboat Trip. A genuine conditions text: risk allocation, cancellation mechanics, conduct rules. The traveler agrees to a document, so the label links the document and the document must be readable in full (section 3).

Flavor 2 · Participation acknowledgment, no document

The three dolphin tours. No conditions text exists; the checklist is about the traveler, not about the operator's liability: height, swimming, pregnancy. So the traveler does not agree to terms, they confirm facts. Short first-person declarations, right at the checkbox, nothing to open. Shown here for Swim with Dolphins in the Lagoon.

Is it the same kind? No, and treating it as one thing would be the mistake.
  • Flavor 1 is a contract text. The traveler accepts the operator's conditions as a document. That needs a canonical, readable, versioned source (section 3) and an "I have read and agree" label that links it.
  • Flavor 2 is a declaration. The traveler attests facts about their own group. There is no document to read, so a document link would be theater. The honest label is "I confirm", and the facts sit directly above the box.
  • It smells like a health and safety declaration because it is one. Flagged to the legal workstream (D1/D2): counsel should classify it, name it, and confirm whether a checkbox declaration is the right instrument or whether some items belong in the operator's on-site waiver instead.
  • The live build's dolphin list also restates platform policy: "cancel or reschedule up to 24 hours" is our own cancellation window (LD1 already renders it in five locked places). Policy restated inside an operator checklist is a second source of truth that can drift. Rule for the CMS: acknowledgment items carry participation facts only, never cancellation, deposit or payment policy.
  • Counsel flag on the old Powerboat document: it bundles a photo release into the required conditions. Consent to marketing use of someone's likeness must be freely given, so it cannot ride along inside a mandatory acceptance. Carried in the D1/D2 hand-off, not reproduced in the placeholder.
  • Same mechanics, different content. Both flavors use the identical required checkbox in the identical place (section 2), so dev builds one gate with two renderings, keyed on a per-tour field (section 4).

Why the commit step wins, honestly weighed
  • The widget's real argument is visibility: the traveler learns early, before any typing. That argument is already served without a checkbox: the LD14 Supplied-by line gains a conditions link (the strip above), and for the dolphin flavor the same facts already render in Not suitable for and Know before you go. Disclosure early, gate once.
  • Acceptance you can prove. At the widget there is no name and no email yet, so a tick there is evidence about a session, not a person. At commit the Contact step is filled and the booking record exists to carry it: timestamp, identity, version (section 4). That is the same acceptance discipline the affiliate terms locked on August 5.
  • One gate that every booking crosses. Refreshes, restored sessions and shared links all funnel through the commit CTA. Server side, the booking is refused when the tour is flagged and no acceptance is on it; the checkbox is the interface to that rule, not the rule.
  • The widget stays as locked. 6.1's job is availability, party, price; LD5 locks the trust strip to exactly two lines; the LD2 CTA progression stays untouched. A legal gate before "Check availability" taxes people who are still comparing, at the moment they are least committed.
  • The cost, named: it lengthens the commit block on flagged tours, and a rejection at the most expensive moment is real friction. Mitigations built in: the box is visible from the moment Payment opens (never springs on tap), the error is one calm line, agreeing inside the reader ticks the box (section 3), and the tour page disclosed it long before.
  • Works on every payment model. On operator_full (C22) the checkbox sits above the bare Reserve my spot CTA the same way; nothing about the pattern assumes a charge.
  • The as-built widget gate also breaks two locks in passing: its Next button sits outside the locked LD2 CTA progression, and a Terms block inside the widget adds a third element to the LD5 two-line trust area. Moving the gate to commit resolves both without a redesign.
  • Payment methods start collapsed, none pre-picked (your call of August 14, flagged as DIT-22). The live build pre-expands Card, and the tall card form pushes iDEAL and PayPal below the fold where they read as fine print. Three closed rows put every method in one glance and let the traveler choose. Named cost: the card majority now taps once more before the fields appear; acceptable here because the list is short, sits above the CTA, and a returning visitor's last method can be pre-picked later (v2 option). This supersedes two locked 5.8 sentences, "Card selected and expanded by default" and "Final CTA lives inside the expanded method" (the CTA stays below the list, always visible); conflict-log entry queued, never silent.
  • Side note, parked: "Not available for USD". The live build renders iDEAL and PayPal as permanently dead rows on a USD session. A dead row at the commit step is friction without information. Recommendation when the pattern is built: follow the wallets rule (a method renders only when it can process the session currency), or keep the row with a hint that switches the currency. Build configuration, not part of this checkbox pattern.
  • For the build review, observed in the same screenshots: the live commit copy reads "By reserving your spot, you agree to our Terms and Privacy policy." where 5.8 locks "By tapping Reserve my spot, you agree to Island Tours' Terms and Privacy Policy.", and a label leaks its spec note ("Postal code (locale-aware)"). Noted here so it rides along to Arnav; not this file's change.

What this settles beyond the pixels
  • New page type for 2.1: per-operator conditions. Only operators with a document get a page; acknowledgment-flavor tours get none (nothing to host). noindex follow, out of the sitemap, linked only from the checkbox label, the LD14 line and the reader.
  • The two migration rows can close on this. /terms-and-conditions/powerboat-caribbean/ and the NL twin, both deliberately held out of redirects.csv, 301 to this page once the route is confirmed. The old page is orphaned on the old site, so the redirect is for bookmarks and link equity hygiene, not traffic.
  • Versioned like the affiliate terms. The page states its version and effective date; the acceptance snapshot on the booking references it. A version bump changes what NEW bookings accept; it never reaches back into old ones.
  • Reduced motion and touch: the layer is opaque, scrolls natively, and mobile gets the full-screen sheet, so the document is genuinely readable on 390, not squinted at in a box.

4 · The data that carries it

Nothing in E.3, E.6 or E.8 knows about operator conditions today (checked against master v1.20). The proposal keeps the document on the operator, the flag and the declarations on the tour, and the evidence on the booking.

Re-acceptancenone storedEvery booking is a fresh acceptance of the version live at commit. The affiliate re-acceptance flow solves a standing account; a booking is a point in time, so nothing to re-accept.

5 · Recommendation, short

Build one gate with two renderings, at the commit step.
  • Placement: the required checkbox sits in the 5.8 commit block, directly above the locked consent line, for both flavors. The widget stays exactly as 6.1 locks it; the tour page discloses early via the LD14 Supplied-by line (document flavor) and the existing content blocks (acknowledgment flavor).
  • Flavor 1, document: "I have read and agree to {operator}'s operator conditions", label links the canonical per-operator page (new 2.1 page type), opened in the flow as a reading layer; Agree inside the reader ticks the box. The two open migration rows 301 to that page.
  • Flavor 2, acknowledgment: a different legal object, so a different rendering: 2 to 6 first-person declarations plus "I confirm this for everyone in my group". No document, no page, no platform policy inside the items. Counsel classifies and names it (D1/D2).
  • Evidence: timestamp, identity and version on the booking record, refused server side without it. The affiliate-terms posture, applied to bookings.
  • Master consequences, flagged not silent: the 5.8 "(links, no checkbox)" lock gains its per-tour exception (DIT-21, conflict-log entry queued), 2.1 gains the page type, E.3/E.6/E.8 gain the fields above. Until the legal workstream lands, every string here is placeholder.

1 · Safety and risk

Powerboat Caribbean takes all reasonable measures to prevent injury and property damage. Speedboat travel involves movement, spray and wave impact; by joining you accept the risks that come with an open powerboat at sea and you follow the crew's safety instructions at all times.

2 · Weather and technical cancellations

If the operator cancels a departure for weather or technical reasons, you choose between a free reschedule and a full refund. This never narrows your Island Tours cancellation window.

3 · Health

This trip is not suitable during pregnancy or with recent back or neck injuries. Limited mobility: message us first, so the crew can advise honestly whether boarding works for you.

4 · On board

  • Follow the crew's instructions at all times.
  • No illegal substances on board.
  • Do not endanger yourself, other guests or the crew.

The crew can end the trip for anyone who makes it unsafe, without refund for that person.

5 · Acknowledgment

By accepting these conditions you confirm the points above for yourself and for everyone you book for.
