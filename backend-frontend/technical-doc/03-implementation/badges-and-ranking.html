Why is the “Most popular” tour ranked 12th?
Because badges describe — rank is bought.

A visual walkthrough of how every tour on a listing gets its badge and its position, using the live Curaçao data. Everything here mirrors the master spec (§3.6 Badges · §3.7 Demand · §7.2 Ranking · §3.8 Diversity).

✓ No logic mismatch — this is the commercial model working as designed

West Point Snorkel & Beach Hop is the best-reviewed tour on Curaçao (★5.0 · 14 reviews → it earns Most popular) — but its operator pays the Boosted tier (25% commission, placement rank 3). Eleven tours ahead of it pay more: one active Spotlight (35%) plus the Premium (30%) and Featured (27.5%) tiers. Ratings never compete across tiers — they only break ties inside the same tier. The same rule puts the well-rated Standard-tier tours (20%) at the bottom: they pay the least, so they rank last, no matter how good their stars are. And so a top card never sits there unexplained, every paid placement without an earned badge wears Sponsored — the honest hint for “this position was bought”. An earned badge is worth more, so it always replaces the hint.

One card, two independent systems

Every tour card is the meeting point of two separate computations. Neither one ever influences the other.

🏅 Badge — “what is true about this tour?”

Max one per card, first match wins. Earned badges lead; Sponsored is the fallback for paid placements with nothing better to show — so a top card always explains itself:

1 Likely to sell out All three, recomputed nightly: tour age ≥ 90 days · ≥ 3 sell-outs in the last 60 days · < 40% seats left over the next 30 days. (~5–10% of catalog — selectivity is the feature.)
2 Most popular ≥ 10 reviews and rating ≥ 4.5. Never granted on commission grounds. Max 1 per category per page (later duplicates fall back to Sponsored when paid).
3 New Published < 30 days ago and 0 reviews. Replaces the rating row on the card.
4 Sponsored Fallback for any paid placement: an active Destination Spotlight (max 3 per island, 35%) or a paid tier P1–P3 (Premium/Featured/Boosted) — shown only when no earned badge applies. It answers “why is this unrated tour at the top?”; an earned badge is more valuable and takes the slot. Open tiers are never labeled.

📍 Position — “how much placement did the operator buy?”

Sorted by commission, refined by quality. Master §7.2. Badges never move a card.

1

Spotlight first is_sponsored DESC

The (max 3) active Spotlight tours lead the whole list — the master’s “labeled placement block”, realized as the top of the grid.

2

Commission tier tier_rank ASC

Premium 30% → rank 1 · Featured 27.5% → 2 · Boosted 25% → 3 · Organic 22.5% → 4 · Standard 20% → 5. This is the dominant key — a higher commission buys a better shelf.

3

Quality score quality_score DESC

0–100, nightly: rating ×40 + review volume ×25 + listing completeness ×20 + conversion ×15. Only breaks ties inside a tier — it can never lift a tour over a better-paying one.

4

Stable tie-break id ASC

Same tier, same quality → fixed order, no shuffling between visits.

Before sorting: unbookable tours are removed entirely (not LIVE, deactivated, or no availability in 30 days — §7.2). After sorting: the diversity pass (§3.8) prevents 3+ same-category tours in a row by pulling the next different-category tour up one or two slots — the small “ripples” you see in the list below.

So how does a great tour climb?

Three levers, in order of impact. Reviews alone only win the fight inside a tier.

Biggest jump

Win a Destination Spotlight

≥ 10 reviews + ★4.5, operator requests, admin approves, max 3 per island. 35% commission while active — the tour leads every listing and wears Sponsored. West Point Snorkel already qualifies.

Sustained placement

Upgrade the commission tier

Paid tiers need the eligibility bar: ≥ 5 reviews, ★4.0, operator 90-day cancellation ≤ 10%. New tours get a one-time 90-day window to hold any tier. Changes lock for 30 days; failing the bar later means 30 days grace, then automatic demotion.

Within the tier

Raise the quality score

More reviews, better rating, complete listing (4+ photos, description, highlights, inclusions, meeting point…). Decides who stands first among equals — never across tiers.

Spec fidelity & current caveats
  • Order, badge triggers, thresholds, bookability filter and diversity pass match the master (§3.6, §3.7, §7.2, §3.8) and the derived docs TOUR-RANKING.md / TOUR-BADGES-AND-RANKING.md. Recorded product decisions on top of master: Spotlight tours lead the list (is_sponsored DESC as sort key #1); Sponsored is the paid-placement fallback badge (earned badges replace it); and each listing’s first card renders pre-highlighted (cream) as the top-placement marker.
  • The q values shown are still the demo-seed placeholders (60 + tier×5, and 0 on two just-published tours). They look inverted across tiers but are harmless — quality only compares tours within one tier. The real nightly quality-score job overwrites them on its next 3:00 UTC run.
  • Badges are recomputed on every request from live data; position ±1 wobbles come from the §3.8 diversity pass, by design.
