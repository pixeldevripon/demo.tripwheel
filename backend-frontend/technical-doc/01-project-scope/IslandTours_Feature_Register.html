0. Read this before you use the register

0.1 Provenance, corrected

Revision 1 got this wrong and I am retracting it. I claimed the requirement set was mostly authored by our side, with Denley appearing only as a vetoing authority, and I advised you to use the authority markers to tell client mandates from our own descopable design opinions. That advice was built on a bad inference and you should ignore it.

What the master's own header line says is "Owner: Denley (founder) · Dev: Arnav · Design: external team." I read "Design: external team" and the presence of files named "Design Review" as evidence that our side produced the specs. In fact Denley's covering messages settle it in the other direction. He wrote the master document. He wrote the specs that sit under it. He produced the login designs, the operator sign-up flow, the onboarding email sequence, the review strategy documents, and the front-end mockups, working with Claude, and he built the Klein Curaçao front-end design himself. "Design: external team" refers to a designer working to him, and the design reviews are him reviewing that designer's wireframes against his own specs.

So the practical position is close to the opposite of what I said. Nearly every line in this register traces to the client. There is very little in here that we could unilaterally descope, and the correct default when a row looks wrong or expensive is to raise it with Denley rather than to treat it as an internal decision.

The authority markers stay, because the distinction they draw is still useful, but it is a narrower distinction than I described. It is about how strong the decision trail is, not about who owns the requirement:

Marker Meaning What you can do with it
FOUNDER Carries a dated founder decision or veto entry in the master's conflict log or Appendix C, or comes directly from one of Denley's messages Cannot change without going back to him. Cite the dated entry when you do
LOCKED Locked in the master as canonical, without a separate dated founder-decision entry against it Still a client requirement. Changing it needs Denley's agreement plus a master version bump. The absence of a dated entry means the audit trail is thinner, not that the requirement is softer
SPEC Specified in a source document that the master routes to as a depth reference Client requirement, one level down in the hierarchy. Denley's rule applies: master wins, specs for depth
PROPOSED Written but explicitly not applied to the master Not yet a requirement. Do not audit against it, and do not treat an audit pass as adopting it
OPEN A named unresolved question Cannot be built or audited until answered. Route it to the named owner

Denley's own precedence rule governs conflicts between the two tiers, and it is worth quoting because it resolves most disputes cleanly: "When in doubt: master wins, specs for depth."
